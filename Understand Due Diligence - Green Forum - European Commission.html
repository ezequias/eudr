Understand due diligence under the EU Deforestation Regulation

Under the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), upstream operators must conduct due diligence to ensure that products are free from deforestation and legally-produced, before placing them on the EU market or exporting from the EU. This webpage provides practical information to help understand what it means to carry out due diligence, what information and sources are used and when downstream verification of due diligence is needed. 

Please use this page in conjunction with the official Guidance, the published FAQs, and the Supply Chain Infographics report. These resources should all be read alongside the EUDR legal text.

Who undertakes due diligence?

Operators upstream in the supply chain, including micro and small primary operators.

When a relevant product is placed on the EU market for the first time or exported from the EU (i.e. not already covered by upstream due diligence), the upstream operator must conduct due diligence. Due diligence is only required for the products in scope of the Regulation (Combined Nomenclature / Harmonised System codes are listed in Annex I of the Regulation). 

To learn more about roles, see the Understand roles and responsibilities webpage. For further information on who undertakes due diligence, see FAQs, including FAQs 3.1 and 5.1.

What does it mean to carry out due diligence?

To comply with the EUDR, operators must have a due diligence system in place to ensure that relevant products have not been produced on land that has been subject to deforestation (or that harvested wood has not induced forest degradation) after 31 December 2020 and that these products have been produced legally in the country of production. 

Exercising due diligence involves three steps:

Step 1: Operators collect the information referred to in Article 9 of the Regulation, such as the commodity or product which they intend to place on the EU market or export, the quantity, supplier, country of production, and evidence of legal harvest, among others.  

A key requirement, in this step, is to obtain the geographic coordinates (geolocations) of the plots of land where the relevant commodity was produced. All the relevant information needs to be put into the due diligence statement or simplified declaration, for submission into the Information System. If the operator cannot collect the required information, it must not place the relevant product on the EU market or export it. 

If after this step, an operator determines that all relevant commodities and relevant products have been produced in countries that are classified as low-risk, then they do not need to continue to steps 2 and 3. These lighter obligations when sourcing from low-risk countries are known as simplified due diligence (as detailed in Article 13 of the Regulation). 

Article 9 information in full
  • a description, including the trade name and type of the relevant products as well as, in the case of relevant products that contain or have been made using wood, the common name of the species and their full scientific name; the product description shall include the list of relevant commodities or relevant products contained therein or used to make those products;
  • the quantity of the relevant products; for relevant products entering or leaving the market, the quantity is to be expressed in kilograms of net mass and, where applicable, in the supplementary unit set out in Annex I to Council Regulation (EEC) No 2658/87 against the indicated Harmonised System code, or, in all other cases, the quantity is to be expressed in net mass or, where applicable, volume or number of items; a supplementary unit is applicable where it is defined consistently for all possible subheadings under the Harmonised System code referred to in the due diligence statement or where provided as part of the simplified declaration;
  • the country of production and, where relevant, parts thereof;
  • the geolocation of all plots of land where the relevant commodities that the relevant product contains, or has been made using, were produced, as well as the date or time range of production; where a relevant product contains or has been made with relevant commodities produced on different plots of land, the geolocation of all different plots of land shall be included; any deforestation or forest degradation on the given plots of land shall automatically disqualify all relevant commodities and relevant products from those plots of land from being placed or made available on the market or exported; for relevant products that contain or have been made using cattle, and for such relevant products that have been fed with relevant products, the geolocation shall refer to all the establishments where the cattle were kept; for all other relevant products of Annex I, the geolocation shall refer to the plots of land;
  • the name, postal address and email address of any business or person from whom they have been supplied with the relevant products;
  • the name, postal address and email address of any business, downstream operator or trader to whom the relevant products have been supplied;
  • adequately conclusive and verifiable information that the relevant products are deforestation-free;
  • adequately conclusive and verifiable information that the relevant commodities have been produced in accordance with the relevant legislation of the country of production, including any arrangement conferring the right to use the respective area for the purposes of the production of the relevant commodity. 
Article 13 information in full

Simplified due diligence:

  • When placing relevant products on the market or exporting them, operators shall not be required to fulfil the obligations under Articles 10 and 11 where, after having assessed the complexity of the relevant supply chain and the risk of circumvention of this Regulation or the risk of mixing with products of unknown origin or origin in high-risk or standard-risk countries or parts thereof, they have ascertained that all relevant commodities and relevant products have been produced in countries or parts thereof that were classified as low risk in accordance with Article 29. In such cases, the operator shall make available to the competent authority upon request relevant documentation demonstrating that there is a negligible risk of circumvention of this Regulation or of mixing with products of unknown origin or origin in high-risk or standard-risk countries or parts thereof.
  • Notwithstanding paragraph 1 of this Article, if the operator obtains or is made aware of any relevant information, including as a result of the assessment carried out under paragraph 1 of this Article, and including substantiated concerns submitted under Article 31, that would point to a risk that the relevant products do not comply with this Regulation or that this Regulation is circumvented, the operator shall fulfil all of the obligations under Articles 10 and 11 and shall immediately communicate any relevant information to the competent authority.
  • Where a competent authority is made aware of any information that would point to a risk of circumvention of this Regulation, including in cases in which relevant commodities or relevant products produced in a standard-risk or high-risk country or a part thereof are subsequently processed in a low-risk country or a part thereof from where they are placed on or leave the market, the competent authority shall take immediate action in accordance with Article 17(1) and, where necessary, adopt interim measures in accordance with Article 23.

Step 2: Operators use the information collected in step 1 to carry out risk assessment. They must verify and evaluate the risk of non-compliant products entering the supply chain, taking into account the criteria described in Article 10 of the Regulation. These criteria include the presence of forests and indigenous peoples, prevalence of deforestation, complexity of the supply chain and strength of law enforcement.

Operators need to show how the information gathered was checked against the risk assessment criteria and how they determined the risk.

Article 10 criteria in full
  • the assignment of risk to the relevant country of production or parts thereof in accordance with Article 29;
  • the presence of forests in the country of production or parts thereof;
  • the presence of indigenous peoples in the country of production or parts thereof;
  • the consultation and cooperation in good faith with indigenous peoples in the country of production or parts thereof;
  • the existence of duly reasoned claims by indigenous peoples based on objective and verifiable information regarding the use or ownership of the area used for the purpose of producing the relevant commodity;
  • prevalence of deforestation or forest degradation in the country of production or parts thereof;
  • the source, reliability, validity, and links to other available documentation of the information referred to in Article 9(1);
  • concerns in relation to the country of production and origin or parts thereof, such as level of corruption, prevalence of document and data falsification, lack of law enforcement, violations of international human rights, armed conflict or presence of sanctions imposed by the UN Security Council or the Council of the European Union;
  • the complexity of the relevant supply chain and the stage of processing of the relevant products, in particular difficulties in connecting relevant products to the plot of land where the relevant commodities were produced;
  • the risk of circumvention of this Regulation or of mixing with relevant products of unknown origin or produced in areas where deforestation or forest degradation has occurred or is occurring;
  • conclusions of the meetings of the Commission expert groups supporting the implementation of this Regulation, as published in the Commission’s expert group register;
  • substantiated concerns submitted under Article 31, and information on the history of non-compliance of operators or traders along the relevant supply chain with this Regulation;
  • any information that would point to a risk that the relevant products are non-compliant;
  • complementary information on compliance with this Regulation, which may include information supplied by certification or other third-party verified schemes, including voluntary schemes recognised by the Commission under Article 30(5) of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 of the European Parliament and of the Council, provided that the information meets the requirements set out in Article 9 of this Regulation. 

Step 3:  Operators take adequate and proportionate measures to mitigate risk, unless under step 2 they find no or only a negligible risk of non-compliance. The risk mitigation measures or procedures must make sure that the risk becomes negligible. The types of measures that might be taken are described in Article 11 of the Regulation.  

For further information on carrying out due diligence, see Guidance, including sections 4, 6 and 8, and see FAQ 5.1.

Measures described in Article 11

 Such procedures and measures may include any of the following: 

  • requiring additional information, data or documents;
  • carrying out independent surveys or audits;
  • taking other measures pertaining to information requirements set out in Article 9.

Such procedures and measures may also include supporting compliance with this Regulation by that operator’s suppliers, in particular smallholders, through capacity building and investments. 

Operators shall have in place adequate and proportionate policies, controls and procedures to mitigate and manage effectively the risks of non-compliance of relevant products identified. Those policies, controls and procedures shall include: 

  • model risk management practices, reporting, record-keeping, internal control and compliance management, including the appointment of a compliance officer at management level for non-SME operators;
  • an independent audit function to check the internal policies, controls and procedures for all non-SME operators. 

How is due diligence documented and confirmed?

Operators need to set up a due diligence system. This is a set of procedures and measures used to make sure that their relevant products meet the requirements of the EUDR. The system must be updated whenever anything important changes, and operators need to review it at least once per year. 

All documents related to due diligence must be kept for five years from the date the product is placed on the EU market or exported. This includes all procedures, records and any updates made to the system, including information gathered under step 1 above. Operators should be able to demonstrate how due diligence was carried out and what mitigation measures are applied when any risk is identified (steps 2 and 3). 

Large operators — meaning businesses who are not SMEs, including microenterprises or natural persons — are also required to publicly report on their due diligence system, including online. Operators who need to carry out value chain due diligence under other EU legislation can meet their EUDR public reporting requirements by including the relevant information in those existing reports.

For further information on documenting due diligence, see FAQs, including FAQs 5.8 and 5.13.

Is there a check-list for due diligence?

Due diligence is not a ‘tick-box’ process, so there is no fixed format or set list of questions that operators must follow. The approach may vary depending on the specific context and supply chain, provided that the different steps of due diligence (collecting information, risk assessment and risk mitigation) are covered. Operators must reach a level of no, or negligible, risk before placing relevant products on the EU market or exporting them.

For further information on due diligence, see FAQs, including FAQ 5.16.

Where do I confirm that I have done due diligence?

Before being placed on the market, relevant products must be covered by a due diligence statement (DDS) or simplified declaration that has been submitted into the EUDR Information System. This is to confirm that due diligence has been done. Annex II of the EUDR legal text provides a template showing the information that needs to be included in DDS. EUDR Annex III provides a template for simplified declarations. These templates are the same for all commodity sectors.

For further information on confirming due diligence, see FAQs, including FAQs 3.3 and 5.19. Find out more about the Information System and how to submit: Guide to the Information System.

When must due diligence take place and how often should it be carried out?

Due diligence must always have been carried out, and a due diligence statement (DDS) or simplified declaration submitted, before relevant products are placed on the EU market or exported.

When is a relevant product produced in the EU considered placed on the market?  

For commodities produced within the EU, the exact date of placing on the market is the point when all three of the following conditions are met:  

  • the product is physically available on the EU market. This means the commodity has been produced, or in the case of processed items, the relevant product has been manufactured
  • the product is supplied on the market for distribution, consumption or use
  • two or more legal or natural persons have agreed that the operator will supply the product. The transaction may involve payment or be free of charge.  

This means that due diligence must have been carried out, the DDS or simplified declaration must have been submitted, and the reference number/declaration identifier must have been obtained before the product is placed on the market. 

For further information on timings, see Guidance, including sections 1 and 4, and see FAQ 5.20.

Do I have to complete due diligence before I import relevant products?  

For relevant products entering the EU market (imports) the reference number of the DDS, or the declaration identifier for simplified declarations, must be made available to customs authorities. The person lodging the customs declaration (known as the customs declarant) must therefore include the DDS reference number or the declaration identifier on the customs declaration lodged for that relevant product.  

This means that due diligence must have been carried out, the DDS or simplified declaration submitted, and the reference number/declaration identifier must be obtained before the lodging of the customs declaration. 

Where a DDS covers multiple shipments/batches the same DDS reference number can be referred to in several customs declarations as long as the legal requirements of the EUDR are respected. It is equally possible to include multiple DDS reference numbers/ declaration identifiers in one customs declaration.  

Do I have to complete due diligence for every batch or shipment of goods?  

A single due diligence statement (DDS) can cover multiple physical batches or shipments of relevant products. For example, where a product is placed on the market successively over a period of time. In these situations, the operator must confirm that due diligence was carried out for all relevant products to be placed on the market (or exported), and once the quantity covered by the DDS has been used, the operator must submit a new DDS for additional quantities. There are additional legal and practical considerations for batch DDS.   

For further information on due diligence statement frequency, see FAQs, including FAQs 5.19 - 5.21.

What if I have carried out due diligence but something changes, or I receive new information?  

Operators must act immediately if they receive new, relevant information — including substantiated concerns — showing that a product they placed on the EU market may not comply with the EUDR. They must inform the Competent Authorities in Member States where the product was placed on the market. They must also inform any downstream operators and traders who received the product. 

For exported products, operators must notify the Competent Authority/ies in the Member State where the product was produced. 

For operators who have carried out simplified due diligence (for products from low-risk countries), if they obtain or are made aware of any relevant information — including substantiated concerns — that would indicate that the relevant products do not comply with the EUDR or that the rules may have been circumvented, the operator must then complete the full due diligence process. This includes carrying out risk assessment and taking risk mitigation measures.  

The operator must also immediately share any relevant information with the relevant Competent Authority/ies. 

For further information on changes/new information, see FAQs, including FAQs 3.11, 3.6.2, 4.15 and 5.1.

Due diligence within company groups

Due diligence obligations relate to legal entities, rather than to company groups. The internal organisation and due diligence policy of a group of companies (a mother company and its subsidiaries) is not governed by the EUDR. The operator that places on the EU market or exports a relevant product is responsible for the compliance of the product and for overall compliance with the Regulation. It is that operator’s name that should be provided in the due diligence statement or simplified declaration and they retain full responsibility under the Regulation. 

For further information on due diligence within company groups, see FAQs, including FAQs 3.13 and 5.3.

Verification of due diligence by downstream operators and traders

When do downstream operators or traders need to verify due diligence?  

The requirement to verify due diligence only applies to downstream operators and traders that are not micro, small or medium-sized enterprises (non-SMEs). 

If they obtain or are made aware of relevant information indicating substantiated concerns that relevant products are not in compliance with EUDR then non-SME downstream operators and traders must verify that due diligence was exercised upstream and that no or only a negligible risk was found.  

Downstream operators and traders shall not place or make available on the market or export relevant products unless the verification demonstrates no or only a negligible risk of non-compliance. 

To learn more about roles, see the Understand roles and responsibilities webpage.

What does it mean to verify upstream due diligence?  

Non-SME downstream operators and traders only need to verify upstream due diligence if there is a substantiated concern. There is no set process for verification, but this could include verifying that the reference numbers and declaration identifiers they have received are valid and checking their content. Non-SME downstream operators or non-SME traders might also wish to collect and analyse additional information. 

For further information on verifying due diligence, see FAQs, including FAQs 3.6, 3.6.1 and 3.6.2.
